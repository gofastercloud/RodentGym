RodentGym Help

RodentGym shows what your Magic Mouse and Magic Trackpad report to the Mac, and what macOS actually delivers to apps. When the two don't match, it tells you.

Reading the window

Device cards. Each connected Magic Mouse or Magic Trackpad gets a card. The drawing uses the sensor's real proportions and shows your fingers live. Blue dots are ordinary touches; orange dots are touches that count as a secondary (right) click under your current settings.

The shaded orange area marks where a secondary click happens. On a Magic Mouse this follows the left/right split and switches sides for left-handed use. On a trackpad it shows the corner you chose, or says secondary click uses two fingers.

Sends. These lights show the raw button reports from the device itself, before macOS processes them.

macOS delivers. These lights show the click events macOS hands to apps. A healthy click lights Sends and then macOS delivers almost immediately.

Under each drawing you'll find the model, firmware, connection, battery, colour and the settings the device's driver is applying right now. Those come from the driver, not from System Settings, so they show what's really in effect.

What the log symbols mean

  • ✓ The click went through normally.
  • ≠ Something changed or didn't match. For example, the mouse sent Right and macOS delivered Left, or a secondary click setting changed.
  • ✗ DROPPED The device reported a click but macOS never delivered it. RodentGym records the memory state at that moment, so you can see whether heavy load was involved.
  • · Information, such as startup, device changes, or a click from a device that sends no button report.

Everything is also written to ~/Library/Logs/RodentGym.log. Choose Devices › Open Log File to read it.

Common findings

Right-click does nothing, and the card says "Secondary click: Off". The mouse is in one-button mode. Turn on secondary click in Mouse settings (Secondary click › Click Right Side). Resetting mouse preferences turns this off.

Right-clicks show as Left in the log (≠). Your finger is landing on the primary side of the split, or another finger is resting on the surface. Watch the dots while you click.

Clicks are DROPPED. The hardware is fine. Something in macOS is discarding the click. Check the memory figures on those log lines, quit anything using a lot of memory or GPU, and restart if it keeps happening. If drops continue on an idle Mac, report it to Apple with Feedback Assistant and include the log lines.

Trackpad clicks show "no button report". Some trackpads don't send a separate button report for every click. RodentGym then matches the click to the trackpad you're touching instead.

Gesture Trainer

A checklist of every gesture you have switched on for each device. Open it with Gym › Gesture Trainer (⌘T). Do a gesture with the pointer over the practice pad and it's ticked off. The banner names what was recognised.

  • App gestures (clicks, tap to click, Force Click, scroll, pinch, rotate, smart zoom, swipe between pages, three-finger drag) are captured on the pad.
  • System gestures (Mission Control, App Exposé, full-screen swipes, squeeze, spread, Notification Centre) are recognised from the raw touches. macOS still acts on them, because RodentGym never blocks input. Come back to the trainer to see the result.
  • Gestures switched off in System Settings show as off. Use the toolbar buttons to open Trackpad or Mouse settings and change them.

Force Sensor

A scrolling graph of how hard you press on a Force Touch trackpad. Open it with Gym › Force Sensor (⌘J) and press with the pointer over the graph.

  • The graph has three bands: Touch (before the click), Click and Force Click. Left clicks are blue and right clicks are orange.
  • A dashed line means the click arrived with no force data. That's normal for a Magic Mouse, which has no force sensor.
  • If presses never reach the Force Click band, check that Force Click is switched on in Trackpad settings.

Cheese Stack

A falling-block game that works every input, so intermittent problems show up while you play. Open it with Gym › Cheese Stack (⌘G).

  • Steer with the pointer. Left click rotates anticlockwise and right click rotates clockwise.
  • Scroll to drop the piece, and flick quickly (or middle-click) to slam it down.
  • Keys: ← → move, ↑ or X rotate clockwise, Z rotate anticlockwise, ↓ drop, Space slam, P pause.

The Left and Right tiles light up as each click arrives. If you click and the tile stays dark, that click didn't reach the app. Dropped clicks counts clicks RodentGym caught going missing during the game.

Settings

  • Keep RodentGym on top keeps the window visible while you click in other apps.
  • Built-in trackpad: Automatic shows a MacBook's own trackpad whenever the lid is open and hides it when the lid is closed. Always show keeps the card, marked "Lid closed" when it's unavailable.
  • Play a sound when a click is dropped makes drops easy to notice.
  • Log every click writes successful clicks as well as problems. Turn it off for long sessions.
  • Drop detection window is how long RodentGym waits for a reported click to arrive before calling it dropped. The default is 400 ms.
  • Device colours. Each accessory reports a colour code, which RodentGym decodes automatically (for example 0 is White, 5 is Grey / space grey, 9 is Space Grey). If a device is drawn in the wrong finish, override it here.

Privacy and permissions

RodentGym only listens. It never changes, blocks or creates input, and needs no Accessibility permission. It watches mouse buttons and touches only, never the keyboard, and nothing leaves your Mac. The log is readable only by you.

Watching mouse click events doesn't normally need any permission. If the macOS delivers lights say "Unavailable", allow RodentGym under Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring and reopen it.

Limitations

  • Touch data comes from Apple's private MultitouchSupport framework. A future macOS update could change or break it.
  • The trackpad corner area is drawn approximately, because Apple doesn't publish its exact size.
  • Colour codes aren't documented by Apple. RodentGym's table matches Apple's Continuity colour codes and has been checked against real devices, but a rare model could be decoded wrongly. Use Settings to override it.
  • Devices are detected when connected and disconnected. If one doesn't appear, choose Devices › Rescan Devices.
